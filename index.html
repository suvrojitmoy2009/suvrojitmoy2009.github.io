C&C Gleeson · Oct 2022 – Present

CRM Modernisation: From Manual Process to Automated System

How a fragmented, Excel-driven supply chain and sales process became a unified, automated Dynamics 365 ecosystem.

40%less manual effort for SMEs
80%faster executive reporting
100%automated daily data refresh
5+ hrssaved per week on syncing

The Problem

When I joined, supply chain and sales processes were spread across disconnected spreadsheets and manual handoffs. Reporting meant pulling Excel files together by hand, data syncing between systems was a manual daily chore, and there was no single source of truth that sales reps or leadership could trust in real time.

The Approach

  • Led the end-to-end design of a Dynamics 365 ecosystem to replace the fragmented legacy setup, acting as the bridge between business stakeholders and IT partners to gather and validate requirements.
  • Built automated workflows with Power Automate to remove repetitive manual steps subject matter experts were doing by hand.
  • Designed automated sync pipelines with Azure Logic Apps so operational data refreshed daily without anyone touching it.
  • Used SQL and Python to validate and clean the underlying data before it fed into reporting or automation — the unglamorous but essential layer underneath everything else.
  • Replaced manual Excel reporting with live Power BI dashboards connected directly to the CRM data, giving leadership real-time visibility instead of a static snapshot.
  • Shipped everything safely through Azure DevOps release pipelines, with proper UAT, defect tracking, and post-go-live support rather than a risky one-shot cutover.

The Outcome

The result wasn't four separate wins — it was one system where automation, clean data, and live reporting reinforced each other. Manual intervention dropped by 40%, executive reporting that used to take hours now takes minutes, and the daily data sync that used to eat 5+ hours of someone's week now runs itself, fully automated, every day.

What I'd Do Differently

Looking back, I'd have introduced the validation and alerting layer for the Logic Apps pipeline earlier — a smaller version of it caught a sync failure quietly for two days before anyone noticed. Now every pipeline I design has a "tell me when you're wrong" step built in from the start, not bolted on afterward.

Jul 2026Power Platform

The Power Platform architecture decision nobody tells you about upfront

When I redesigned our Dynamics 365 setup, the hardest choice wasn't which tool to use — it was where the logic should live. Power Automate, Azure Logic Apps, and Power Apps can all technically do the same job, and early on I put things in the wrong layer more than once. The rule I follow now: Power Automate for anything business-user-facing and easy to hand off, Logic Apps for anything system-to-system that needs to be resilient and monitored, and Power Apps only for the actual UI. Getting that split wrong is invisible for months — until someone needs to change a "simple" flow and discovers it's quietly become the backbone of three other processes.

Jun 2026Agentic AI

What "agentic" actually means once you've shipped one

Everyone's calling everything an agent right now, and honestly it's making the term less useful. After building an AI feature inside our Dynamics 365 setup that cut sales rep workload by 30%, here's the line I now use: if it can only answer when asked, it's a chatbot. If it can decide what to do next, check its own work, and only loop you in when something's genuinely ambiguous, that's agentic. Most "AI agents" I see in demos are the first thing wearing the second thing's name.

The boring part of GenAI is where the value actually is

Nobody gets excited writing a prompt that summarizes a support ticket. But that's the part that saves five hours a week, not the flashy demo. The lesson I keep relearning in CRM work: the unglamorous, repetitive task your team complains about quietly in standups is almost always the best candidate for automation — not the impressive-looking edge case everyone wants to showcase.

Apr 2026Data Pipelines

Why I trust automation more after building it badly once

My first Azure Logic Apps pipeline broke quietly for two days before anyone noticed the sync had failed. That was a useful kind of embarrassing. Now every pipeline I design has a "tell me when you're wrong" step built in from day one — alerts, validation checks, a human checkpoint somewhere. The goal of automation isn't removing people from the loop; it's making sure the loop only needs them when it actually matters.
